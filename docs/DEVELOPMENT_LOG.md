# PokerTrainer — Development Log

This is a concise record of major decisions and verification, not a claim that every planned feature is finished.

1. **Concept and prototype.** Built an offline SwiftUI Hold'em game for solo practice and short play sessions. Replaced real-money language and “GTO AI” claims with chips and an honest description of a probabilistic, rule-based opponent.
2. **Core review.** Reproduced skipped betting responses, all-in and unmatched-chip errors, overstated tie equity, folded-card disclosure, and stale asynchronous calculations.
3. **Heads-up stabilization.** Moved betting and settlement into a deterministic engine. Added turn, card, chip, and save-state invariants with regression tests.
4. **Three-seat table.** Added circular action order, dealer/blind rotation, main and side pots, short-all-in rules, and two independently acting computer opponents.
5. **Local continuation.** Added separate atomic, versioned saves for both tables. A loaded game is checked before restoring cards, deck, actor, chips, and campaign progress.
6. **Difficulty and campaigns.** Added a context-aware opponent policy, distinct playing personalities, and four-level progression in both modes. The three-seat engine can continue with two active seats after an elimination.
7. **Extended hands-on validation.** Won the 1 vs 1 championship on an iPhone 17 Simulator. Early 1 vs 2 play covered all-ins, folds, showdowns, elimination, two-seat continuation, save/relaunch, and app updates. This revealed rebuy and fold-pot-display defects, which were corrected and given regression tests.
8. **Pacing review.** A long 1 vs 2 run reached hand 153 while still at Level 2 with fixed 10/20 blinds. Blinds now scale by level (10/20, 20/40, 40/80, 80/160), beginning at the next hand for an existing save.
9. **Live-equity correction.** Direct play found that the previous street's equity could remain visible while the next street was being calculated. Both tables now show a calculation state until the new estimate is ready; the three-seat table also recalculates when a fold changes the number of active players.
10. **Tournament pacing.** Within each 1 vs 2 level, blinds double every 12 completed hands, capped after 60 hands. An extended Level 4 duel showed that the previous 24-hand cap could still stall a campaign. Older campaign saves start the blind clock at zero; their current hand and chips remain intact.
11. **Shared-session boundary.** A later interactive Simulator session visibly reached Level 4. Because another person was operating that same Simulator during the final segment, that result is not treated as a controlled solo playthrough or a verified 1 vs 2 championship.
12. **Controlled 1 vs 2 championship.** On a separate iPhone Simulator, I directly played random deals through Levels 1–4 without another person touching the device. Earlier attempts lost in Levels 2–4, exposing how misleading random-hand equity can be against a selective opponent. The final uninterrupted campaign won at hand 106: both Level 4 opponents had zero chips, the player had 21,000, and the championship message appeared. The isolated save survived an app update and relaunch.
13. **Action and presentation polish.** Clamped both raise presets to the engine's minimum legal raise. Added view-only card arrival, showdown flip, chip-contribution, and pot-award effects, disabled by Reduced Motion. Initial iPhone 17 and smaller iPhone 17e Simulator checks confirmed both table layouts, hidden cards before showdown, revealed cards afterward, and continued betting. Full animation-timing and Reduced Motion UI review is still pending.
14. **Reproducible difficulty baseline.** Added a seedable equity estimator and opt-in 1 vs 1/1 vs 2 benchmarks using the real rule engines. Across 48 identical deals per level against a fixed check/call player, with 24 equity samples per decision, player chip changes for Levels 1–4 were -80/-318/-820/-1700 in 1 vs 1 and -226/-496/-668/-1430 in 1 vs 2. Both modes showed the expected ordering in this narrow test, not proof of generally stronger play; this motivated the wider checks below.
15. **Small-screen and motion review.** On an iPhone 17e Simulator, I enabled Reduce Motion and directly played both table layouts through actions, community-card reveals, and a 1 vs 2 showdown. The setting suppressed presentation effects without blocking play and was returned to its original state. The 1 vs 1 status message and five betting buttons were cramped; I gave the status full width, split the buttons into two rows, and set a 44-point minimum button height in both modes. The updated build was visually checked in both modes and resumed the same hand after a Home-screen return.
16. **Long sessions and wider opponent checks.** An opt-in stress test completed 500 hands in each mode, restoring the local save after all 3,334 heads-up and 4,506 three-seat actions. Paired-deal benchmarks now include passive, selective, and pressure-playing opponents. The original Level 4 policy overused thin value bets in a short-estimate heads-up matchup, so I raised its heads-up value threshold while preserving the multiway threshold. Against a selective player over 192 paired heads-up deals with 24 equity samples per decision, the player's chip change versus Level 4 moved from -455 to -915; Level 3 was -1044, so strict ordering is not established for every matchup. These are controlled policy diagnostics, not evidence of professional-level poker strength.
17. **Production-sample follow-up.** Against the selective policy over 16 paired deals per level, using the app's opponent decision sample counts, player chip changes for Levels 1–4 were +114/+54/-110/-285 in 1 vs 1 and -150/-240/-651/-673 in 1 vs 2. Both sequences have the intended ordering, but 16 deals and one fixed opponent are far too narrow to claim a general difficulty ranking.
18. **Physical-device smoke test.** Installed a signed Debug build on an iPhone 17 running iOS 26.7 and directly played for approximately 16 minutes. In 1 vs 2, Level 1 ended with both opponents eliminated, Level 2 began at its higher blinds, and a further hand completed. In 1 vs 1, play reached Level 1 Hand 8. Background return and force-quit/relaunch restored the same in-progress cards, board, pot, and chip stacks in both modes; the two campaigns remained separate. Sampled completed states preserved the 3,000-chip and 2,000-chip table totals, respectively. No crash or freeze was visible in this bounded session. Some chip digits briefly overlapped during animation frames but settled correctly, so timing still needs review. The phone remained on charge at the user's request: battery drain, unplugged thermal behavior, and a quantitative power trace were not measured. The session stopped when the user resumed using the phone.
19. **Creator feedback and balance revision.** In a later play session, the creator reported 94% displayed battery at both 12:06 and 12:23, with almost no perceived heat. On the then-current build, they cleared the entire 1 vs 1 campaign in about 10 minutes and reached mid-Level 3 in 1 vs 2 in about 5 minutes. I made the early opponents less passive, scaled heads-up blinds by level, and set new-level opponent stacks against the player's carried chips in both modes. Saved in-progress hands retain their existing state. Short paired-deal benchmarks improved several early-level matchups, but some 1 vs 2 level orderings remain close; the changes do not establish professional-level or generally difficult play.
20. **Training and table feedback.** Added MENU access during active hands, a tap-to-open coach beside the player's hole cards, clearer card faces and dealing, chip contributions, and a one-second pot-to-winner payout. A result banner identifies the winner, awarded chips, and revealed winning rank. The coach explains made hands, direct draws, upgrade paths, call price, and a conservative tip from visible information; it is not a GTO solver or a read of hidden cards. A new Simulator playthrough exposed a misleading case in which a folded player's cards could form a strong hand after the board completed; the UI now marks that player ineligible instead of presenting the rank as a playable hand. Coach regression tests also distinguish shared-board flush/straight threats from private draws. A recorded iPhone 17e Simulator hand visibly showed chips leaving the pot for Opponent 2 and the result banner; mode switching and saved-hand restoration were also checked. At this point, the updated build had not yet been installed on the physical iPhone.
21. **Portfolio checkpoint and current-device install.** Built the current code for the connected iPhone 17, confirmed its existing development-team signature, installed it over the previous app without uninstalling, and launched its bundle successfully. The creator reported no further issues after installation. No timed play session, new full-campaign victory, or quantitative power/thermal measurement was recorded for this build. Feature work is frozen for the portfolio milestone; further changes should address reproduced defects or separately scoped future goals.

**Current verification:** 78 passing default core tests, two passing opt-in benchmark cases, a passing 500-hand-per-mode save/restore stress test, successful iOS Simulator and signed physical-device builds, full Simulator championships on the earlier build, the bounded earlier physical-device session, updated iPhone 17e Simulator UI checks, and installation/launch of the current build on an iPhone 17. The later 17-minute battery reading changed by zero displayed percentage points, not a measured zero energy draw. Broader human-play validation remains optional future work; none of these checks proves the app is bug-free.
